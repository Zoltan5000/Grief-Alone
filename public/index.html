Guide

For the person calling. Start small. There's no right way to begin.

  • "Hi, I'm not really sure what to say. Is it okay if we just talk?"
  • "Who did you lose? What was their name?"
  • "Would you tell me a little about them?"
  • "How has today been for you?"
  • "What do you miss most about them?"
  • "What's something people don't understand about your grief?"
  • "Is there a memory of them that still makes you smile?"
  • "I just needed to talk to someone who gets it."

For the person answering. You don't need to fix anything. Being there is enough.

Do

  • Let them lead. Follow where they want to go, even if it's not about grief.
  • Leave room for silence. Count to five before you fill a pause.
  • Say their person's name if you know it. Many people long to hear it.
  • Reflect back what you hear: "That sounds exhausting." "You really loved her."
  • Share a little of your own story only if it helps them feel less alone, then turn back to them.

Try not to say

  • "They're in a better place."
  • "Everything happens for a reason."
  • "I know exactly how you feel."
  • "At least…" (anything that starts this way)
  • "You should be feeling better by now." Grief has no deadline.

Look after yourself

  • It's okay to say "I need to stop here" and end the call kindly.
  • Take a moment before opening your line again.

If they say they want to die or aren't safe

Stay calm. It's okay to ask directly: "Are you thinking about ending your life?" Asking does not put the idea in their head. Encourage them to contact a crisis line or emergency services now. You are not expected to handle this alone.
